# Project state

Last updated: 2026-11-25, after Review and the sideline coach were reworked (1.7e).

## Summary

Roadmap phase 1 (coached game MVP) is about half done. You can play a full game against a Maia opponent at your level. The coach speaks on rule-based triggers (tactic available, threat, mistake with takeback offer, praise, phase prompts) and gives 4-step hints, all from board-checked facts. The coach also thinks aloud on its own moves (how it answered your threat, its captures, the idea behind a quiet move), honestly and under the comment budget. Two modes: Lesson (all of the above, takebacks) and Sparring (serious moments only, hints on request, no takebacks, no thinking aloud). You can view earlier positions. You can "Try a sideline": play a move or a short line on the board without committing, and the coach answers with Stockfish, says after each of your moves whether it holds up, and names the concept behind it (or says it can't). After a mistake, "Show me" plays the coach's answer. Games are saved on the device and resume after a reload; finished games, their moments and sidelines are kept and can be exported as PGN or backed up to a file. You can import games (pasted PGN, a .pgn file, or your latest games from Chess.com or Lichess): the coach looks for the same moments as in a live game and the games appear in History and backups. History has a Review button on each game: the game opens on the board, the coach looks through the whole game once (both sides, every pattern, whatever the level) and walks through its biggest moments in the past tense, with a strip showing how the evaluation moved. In a sideline the coach words every move of the line, and "Show what's good" plays the engine's best line with a note for each move. Not yet built: plans (free text) and Practice.

## Feasibility results (desktop only so far)

Measured on the developer's PC (16 cores, Intel Iris Xe-class integrated GPU).

- **Stockfish 19 Lite, single-threaded WASM:** ~1.9 MB, loads in ~0.1 s, depth 18 in under 0.7 s. MultiPV works (used for "forced" checks). Strength options exist for the fallback opponent.
- **Maia3 5M (maia3-js, ONNX wasm):** 22.6 MB model, ~23 ms per move. Plays clearly weaker as rating drops down to ~600 Lichess; at 400 it becomes erratic (out of training range). Levels use 600 as the floor.
- **WebLLM coach voice:** Llama 3.2 1B made up facts; Qwen3.5 2B was close (5–10 s per comment, good at JSON plan extraction, but mixed up whose piece is whose); Qwen3 4B was better but too slow (~2 tokens/s). Conclusion: templates are the main voice; a model may rephrase or extract structure only.

## Decisions (differ from or add to the spec)

These should be folded into the spec (see PLAN, task "Spec sync").

1. **Templates are the coach's main voice**, not the in-browser model (feasibility test 3). The model's role: rephrase templates, extract structured claims from typed plans, persona flavour.
2. **Opponent rating floor is 600 Lichess** (Maia erratic below ~500). Level 1 is made easier by the coach, not a weaker Maia.
3. **Line cutting rule 1 ("stays changed")** = material holds for 4 plies after the cut. Losses later in the engine line are reported honestly ("you may give some of it back").
4. **Line cutting rule 2 ("nothing left to settle")** = no capture pending. Checks or threats after the cut only block it if they win material back (spite checks are ignored).
5. **"Loose piece" (Level 1 piece safety)** = a capture, optionally followed by a recapture: covers undefended pieces and pieces attacked by a cheaper one.
6. **Patterns per level** (`levels.ts`): L1 loose piece, mate in 1; L2 + fork, mate in 2; L3 + discovered attack, general material wins; L4+ + longer mates. Pins and skewers aren't detected yet.
7. **Licensing:** Stockfish and chessground are GPL-3.0, so the app will be GPL open source in a public repo (also fits free GitHub Pages).
8. **Games can start from any position** (`?fen=` for now), the base for "start from repertoire positions".
9. **Thinking aloud (task 1.3):** the coach narrates (a) the player's threat (a teachable line from the analyst's "pass" check) and how its move answered it, only when the engine confirms the threat is gone; (b) its captures (recapture, free piece, en passant, with check) and promotions; (c) quiet-move ideas (castling, developing, a centre pawn) in the first 24 plies, each at most once or twice a game. It **never rates its own move**: no "good/best/strong" wording at all, and no purposeful-sounding reason for a move that lost the level's mistake threshold (move quality vs Stockfish is computed in `narration.ts`). Its own new threats aren't narrated: within the player's level the existing "what does my last move want?" question covers them, beyond it they stay silent (rule 3). Candidate moves ("I considered…") aren't narrated yet.
10. **Narration budget:** a threat it answered may be said 2 plies after the last remark, a capture 4, a quiet idea 6 (the general budget). Narration and the player-turn remark share one bubble (`joinRemarks`).
11. **Modes (task 1.4).** The mode is a set of switches in `modes.ts`; changing it applies from the next game (like the level). Sparring:
    - **Silent:** the unasked prompts ("There's something strong here", "what does my last move want?"), phase prompts, "Good, you saw my threat", and all thinking aloud on its own moves (its captures and ideas count as revealing its plans; the moves speak for themselves).
    - **Kept as serious moments:** a mistake within the player's level is flagged without naming the refutation or drawing an arrow ("Careful: Bc4 gives me a chance to win material." / "…to checkmate you."); a missed tactic ("You had something stronger than a3 here."); short praise for finding a tactic.
    - **Hints** only from the Hint button, starting at step 1 (in Lesson the tactic prompt counts as step 1).
    - **No takebacks:** the button is hidden, no offer, and the loss message doesn't suggest taking back.

12. **Hung material is flagged below the mistake size (Lesson).** Found in play: at Level 2, 6.d4? in `rnb1kb1r/ppp2ppp/8/3qp3/8/3P4/PPP2PPP/R1BQKBNR w KQkq - 0 6` just drops a pawn (−0.42 → −1.73), but the coach was silent because the level's mistake size is 2.5 pawns and its smallest taught gain 3. Now a move is also flagged when (a) the engine's best reply wins material as a loose piece or pawn (`hanging-piece` pattern: a capture, maybe a recapture, at least 1 pawn), **and** (b) the evaluation drops by at least 0.9 pawns per pawn won (`HUNG_CP_PER_PAWN`; first 0.7, raised because Stockfish rates the Evans Gambit 4.b4 0.74 and the King's Gambit 2.f4 0.67 worse than the best move). (b) keeps even trades, gambits and sacrifices quiet. Same "Careful: after d4, I can play exd4…" remark and takeback offer as a mistake. Sparring skips it (`Mode.minorMistakes`): it flags only the level's real mistakes. A sound sacrifice or gambit (the evaluation holds) is deliberately not called a mistake; an unsound one is flagged like any hung material. The engine's own top move is never flagged (evaluations of sharp positions jump between searches).
13. **Why taking back fails.** When the refutation is a single capture of a defended piece, the mistake remark says why the player can't just take back, if board code shows it (`failedRecapture` in `facts.ts`): exactly one legal recapture, the coach takes the recapturing piece on the same square, the player can't take again, and the recapturing piece is worth more than what it won. "That would win a pawn: if you recapture with Qxd4, I take your queen with Qxd4." Longer exchanges, two ways to take back, or reasons elsewhere on the board get no "why" (attacker/defender counting is a possible next step).
14. **Praise for a sound sacrifice (Lesson).** So the player learns when a move that gives material is good. Two checks: (a) board facts: the move *offers* material, i.e. the coach can legally take the moved piece and the player can't win back as much on that square (their own capture plus at most one recapture of the taker), at least a pawn net; (b) the engine: the move is its top move or within 0.6 pawns of it (`FOUND_CP`). "Bold! Bxh7+ offers your bishop for a pawn, but the engine rates it among the best moves here. Can you see what you get for it?" Whether the engine would accept the offer doesn't matter (for the Greek gift it sometimes declines with Kh8). Found tactics within the level keep their own praise. Pawn breaks retaken at once (Italian 5.d4 exd4 cxd4, Morra 3.c3 dxc3 Nxc3) and gambits worth less than the best move (Evans, King's Gambit) get nothing.

15. **Explain your idea (task 1.5) — superseded by decision 18 (arrow entry removed in 1.6d; the line checks in `linecheck.ts` live on, one move at a time).**
    - **Live position only.** Explain is disabled while viewing an earlier position, and arrow mode blocks move navigation. `LineInput` takes any `rootFen`, so 1.6 can lift this and start a line (or branch) from a viewed position.
    - **Line state = `Line { rootFen, moves: LineMove[] }`** (`lines-input.ts`); each `LineMove` has from/to/promotion/uci/san and `mine`. The player's moves are the ones at even index (the player moves first); the opponent's replies are entered by tapping their pieces too. `LineInput` has `undo`, `truncate(n)` and `load(moves)`, which is what branches need (keep a prefix, fork). The game screen keeps the last checked line (`lastLine`) for "Change my line". For 1.6: a branch can start from `input.line`; the verdict is a pure function of the line (`checkLine`), so a branch can reuse it per move.
    - **Max 8 moves** per line (checking costs one search per position; the longer "play it out" case is the branch of 1.6).
    - **Verdict rules** (`linecheck.ts`, one Stockfish search per position, the value of a move = the negated score after it, compared with the best move's score): first problem along the line decides. (a) *Refuted at move N*: the player's move is worth ≥1.5 pawns less than the best move **and** leaves them under +1.0 (a weaker move that keeps an edge isn't "refuted"; the good-line text mentions the stronger move instead). The "why" is the opponent's best reply, with what it wins (taught line, `winsPhrase`) or else the engine score. (b) *The opponent has a better reply*: their move in the line is worth ≥1.5 pawns less than their best, so the line doesn't force it; the better reply and both scores are given. (c) *Good line*: nothing above; says what it wins/mates and the engine score. Ignored on purpose: a mate that is only slower, and differences when both moves leave a side at +5.0 or worse.
    - **Honesty:** wording says "the engine agrees / rates", never "correct". Scores are always labelled for whom (`+1.4 for you`).
    - **Lesson:** a tactic prompt also shows a "Show me your idea" button (`Remark.offerExplain`); the Explain button is always there (also in Sparring: the player asks for it, so it isn't unasked talk). Not done: asking for an explanation at other key moments.
    - Arrows: green = your move, blue = their reply, numbered; after a verdict the board keeps the arrows up to the failing move, with the better reply in red.

16. **Branches (task 1.6a) — superseded by decision 18 (one level, no stack, no breadcrumb).** `BranchStack` holds at most 2 `Branch`es over chess.js; the game is not touched while one is open (no takebacks, hints, Explain, or navigation inside). Start: the shown position (live, or an earlier one when viewing; the player keeps their colour, so if the opponent is to move there the coach plays first). The coach's reply is Stockfish's top move (analyst, depth 12/10), not Maia. Coach text in a branch is limited to board facts (start, checkmate/draw); it is kept in the branch (`Branch.notes`), not in the game's remark log. Lesson only (`Mode.branches`). No clock exists yet, so "clock pauses" is moot.

17. **Design reset (Nov 23).** The product is one loop: moment → idea or sideline → engine-checked consequence → named concept → record → comes back in practice. Same coach and tools in the live game, Review (imported or saved games) and Practice. The user's gripe with engines (they give the best move, not why) is the core: lines must be translated to **concepts**, detected by board code and confirmed by the engine, never invented; when no detector fires the coach says it can't name a reason. Concept names and test data come from the Lichess puzzle themes. Details in the spec, "The idea behind the product".
18. **Try a sideline replaces branches and the arrow flow.** One level deep, no merging, no nested branches, no breadcrumb; judged move by move; open (no limits) in Lesson, absent in Sparring (cheating the coach only cheats the learner). Task 1.6a's two-deep `BranchStack` and "What if?" button are a sandbox with no teaching value and get reshaped in 1.6d; the arrow entry of 1.5 is retired there. Decision 16 is superseded.
19. **Rule 4 relaxed for game import:** a player-started fetch of their own public games from the Chess.com or Lichess API is allowed (the username is sent to that service only). Cross-origin support is to be checked in 1.7c. CLAUDE.md updated.

20. **How the sideline judges and words (task 1.6d).** After each player move the coach runs `checkLine` on that one move (engine value of the move vs the best move there, same thresholds as before: 1.5 pawns lost = refuted unless the player still stands ahead, 0.6 = mention the stronger move) with an engine cache so the reply search and the judging share one search. Words: the engine's call first ("The engine agrees with…", "…holds up, though the engine would pick…", "…doesn't hold up. I'd answer with …, winning …"), then the concept from `explain.ts` (the move that is *worded*: the player's move when it holds up, the coach's refuting reply when it doesn't). Each concept sentence states only what the detector checked ("attacks my king on e8 and my rook on a8 at once", "pinned to the king and can't leave that line"). A spotting habit follows the first time a concept is named in a game. If the engine refutes but no concept is found: "I can't name a reason I can check." Scores are shown only in that fallback, since "+2.3" means nothing to the target players. Not yet worded: trapped piece, sacrifice, capturing the defender, deflection and the rest (their detectors are below the precision bar, see Known issues).
21. **"Show me" and "Try it out".** In Lesson, a mistake remark offers Take back / Show me / Play on. Show me opens a sideline from the game position (the coach to move) and plays its best reply with a short description; Back to game returns to the three choices. A tactic prompt offers "Try it out".

22. **What is saved, and how (task 1.7).** localStorage, on this device only: `sideline.game.<id>` per game (JSON `SavedGame`: start FEN, colour, level, mode, UCI moves, the coach's remarks with their ply, sidelines, moments, hints used, result), `sideline.index` (newest first, at most 100; the oldest go first, never the game in progress), `sideline.current` (the unfinished game). A game with no move is not saved, and a takeback to the start forgets the saved game, so a reload never brings back a taken-back position. The engine's analysis is not saved: on resume the coach re-analyses silently ("Welcome back. Your move."), or the opponent moves again if it was its turn. `?fen=` always starts a new game. A sideline in progress is not saved; one that was tried is kept (as a PGN variation) unless a takeback removes its position. The browser is asked to keep the data (`navigator.storage.persist()`). Storage is per address: `localhost:5173`, `:5178` and the deployed site are separate.
23. **Moments (the record the improvement loop reads).** A `Moment` is attached to a mistake, a missed tactic or a found tactic: the position the player decided in (FEN), their move, the coach's answer (`better`), the concept (the worded detector, else the motif the line was found under, as a Lichess-style theme; none when neither names one), the cost in centipawns, and, saved with it, the ply, the coach's words, what the player chose (taken back or played on) and whether they asked "Show me". Moments survive a takeback (a taken-back mistake is still a mistake). Sacrifice praise, "Good, you saw my threat" and narration are not moments.
24. **Game import (task 1.7c).** Ways in: pasted PGN, an uploaded .pgn, or the player's latest 20 standard games from Lichess or Chess.com, started by a button; nothing is fetched otherwise. The username goes only to that service; the last username per service is remembered in localStorage (`sideline.import.names`) so the next fetch and side-guessing for pasted games work. Only the main line is read (comments, variations, clock tags and NAGs in a file are dropped: the engine decides what is true). A file or a service answer is cut into games; unreadable games are reported by number and skipped; only standard chess is read. The player picks which games to analyse (the first 5 of a fetch are ticked) and which side they played (guessed from usernames, else asked: a wrong side would give wrong moments). Imported games go through `Coach` itself (`reviewGame` in `review.ts`: `onPlayerTurn` then `onPlayerMove` for each of the player's moves), so a mistake, a missed tactic and a found tactic are judged by the live rules at the level chosen in the dialog (default: the Level select). The mode is `REVIEW` (`modes.ts`, not in the Mode select): Lesson's rules without prompts, small talk or takebacks, so the moment text has no takeback offer. A saved game has `mode: 'review'` and `imported` (source, names, date, site, Elo, time control: written back out in the PGN). Ids are a hash of the position, moves, side and names, so the same game is recognised as "already saved" and not analysed twice; the other side of the same game is a separate record. Opponent moves are not analysed. Moment texts kept the live wording at the time; decisions 28–29 replace that (Review words them in the past tense).
25. **How a whole game is analysed without freezing the page (task 1.7c).** The engine already runs in a Web Worker, so the page's thread is free; what had to be decided is queueing, feedback and cancelling. (a) **A second Stockfish** for the import (`Analyst.create()`, terminated when done) instead of the live analyst: a game in progress keeps its instant hints and the import is not stuck behind them. (b) **One game at a time, one move at a time:** the loop awaits each of the player's moves (about 3 searches each) and checks the cancel signal before the next, so Cancel takes effect within about a second. (c) **Progress:** "Game 2 of 5 · move 14 of 40" and a bar over the whole batch; the dialog is modal (no playing meanwhile). (d) **Cancel** (button, Esc or closing the dialog) keeps the games already finished and drops the half-finished one (never a half-analysed record). Each game is saved as soon as it is done, so a closed tab loses at most one game. (e) **`ucinewgame` before each game** (`Analyst.newGame`): found in testing that reviewing the fork position after another game gave a different engine answer (O-O-O instead of Nxc7+, no tactic found) because the search carries hash and move-ordering state between searches; a game reviewed after another now gets the same answers as alone. The same state effect exists inside a game and in live play; it is not removed there (it would cost the speed that sharing gives). (f) Depth is the analyst's usual one (12, or 10 on touch devices). Measured: about 10 s for 204 moves on a desktop CPU with the emulated phone's depth; real phones are untested. No move cap yet: a fetch ticks only 5 games, and the button shows how many of the player's moves are selected.
26. **Review (task 1.7d).** A saved game (imported or played here) opens on the game screen: same board, move list, navigation and Try a sideline, in the `REVIEW` mode, with moves blocked (`this.reviewing`). The moments (`sortedMoments`) are walked with ‹ Moment / Moment ›; each one shows the position **before** the player's move, and the coach asks what they were thinking ("Here you played 5.Bc4. What were you thinking?…") without saying whether it was good. **What happened?** moves to the position after and shows what was saved then (the coach's words and arrows from the game's remark log). The kind of moment appears in the bar only after that. Sidelines tried in a review are saved with the game and show up as PGN variations at that move; nothing else is written (no result, no current-game pointer, no re-ordering from just viewing). The game in progress is not touched: Back to my game resumes it like a page reload (or starts a new game when there is none). The "what were you thinking" answer is not collected: it is a question to think about, not text input (free-text plans belong with the plans task). The mistake wording is the saved live wording ("Careful: after Bc4…"), still a little odd for a past game.
27. **Known openings (task 1.7b).** The book is a lookup of 3,865 named positions; "in the book" means *exactly this position is a named line*. Effects: (a) a move that leaves a position in the book is not flagged as hung material below the level's mistake size and gets no "Bold!" praise; real mistakes within the level are still flagged. (b) A move that enters a gambit (the position after it has a name containing "Gambit", the position before it was not that gambit) **and** offers material by the board facts of decision 14 is named: "That's the Queen's Gambit, a known opening: you offer a pawn." plus "The engine prefers other moves here." only when the move is at least 0.9 pawns worse than the engine's best, plus the pointer "We'll look at this one in openings training." (Lesson only; there is no such training yet). A "Gambit" name without an offer (e.g. 2...e6 in the Queen's Gambit Declined) is silent. (c) The coach playing a gambit says so every time it enters one when thinking aloud (every game; also after a takeback replays it; Lesson, Sparring silent): "I'm playing the Evans Gambit, a known opening: I offer a pawn." It does not rate its move. (d) `SavedGame.opening` = the deepest named position of the game ({eco, name, ply}), for games from the normal start; set on every save of a live game and on import; shown in History next to the move count. Review also uses the book (no flagged book moves in imported games).
28. **Review looks at everything; the level only sets the cutoff (task 1.7e).** Rule 3 governs the live coach; Review was asked for, so it checks every pattern at any level, for both sides, with one engine search per position (`reviewGame`, `coach/review.ts`). The level's `mistakeCp` is the smallest loss listed, its `minGain` the smallest win that counts as a tactic; a pattern outside the level's motifs is tagged `beyond` and said so in the text; the shortest teachable line is shown (the level's length first). At most 8 moments are kept per game, costliest first (found tactics rank low), so Review stays a short lesson. Kinds: the player's `mistake` (with the opponent's winning line as `answer`, the better line as `line`; a big drop with no pattern says "I can't name a reason I can check"), `missed-tactic`, `found-tactic`, and the opponent's `their-tactic` (not when the player's move before already allowed exactly it) and `their-blunder` (dropped when the player's next move is itself a moment, which is then marked `prompted`). A record without `curve` is analysed when Review opens it (live-played games too; the live thinking-aloud remarks and `outcome`/`showedMe` stay).
29. **Words for the past, from facts (task 1.7e).** A saved moment holds the facts (`fen`, `played`, `line`, `answer`, `concept`, `lossCp`) and `coach/reviewwords.ts` words them in the past tense about both sides ("You played 5.Bc4. After that, rival can play Nxd4, winning a queen for a knight."), via `explainLine`. The habit sentence comes from the concept sentence actually shown, not from the recorded concept. The question "what were you thinking?" is dropped (nothing collected): the coach says "Look at the position first: what would you do? Try your idea in a sideline, or tap What happened?". After the answer: Show the line (sideline with the better line played out) and Try it yourself. A plain recapture is not a tactic (`isMereRecapture`: the line's gain counted from before the opponent's last capture is below the level's minimum), in the live coach (`Coach.previous`, set from the coach's own move) and in Review. `trappedPieces` no longer reports a piece as trapped while the side is in check (found in play: it said "traps their bishop on d4" after Rxc8+).
30. **Line-level wording (task 1.7e).** `explainLine(root, ucis, {player, voice})` returns one step per move: `conceptNote` for the first-move concepts, else plain facts (a capture, a take-back, a check), plus pay-offs that keep the squares the detectors checked: the skewered / pinned / trapped piece that is later taken, a defender that left so that what it defended is taken (deflection), a king that took an offered piece followed by check (attraction), a check or capture played before taking back (intermezzo). Each sentence states only what the board code checked and does not claim a move was forced. In a sideline every move gets a note (the player's verdict adds a pay-off; the coach's reply is worded, only its pay-off when the verdict already named it); "Show what's good" (`Coach.bestLine`) plays the engine's line cut to the taught part (else 3 plies) with its result ("It wins a bishop.", "ends in checkmate", or the engine's score and "I can't name a pattern in it that I can check"). Voices: `coach` (my / your) in sidelines, `review` (their / your) in the saved texts.

## Known issues and gaps

- **Detector precision against Lichess labels is a proxy, not truth.** Many "false positives" are true geometry that Lichess did not tag (an incidental double attack is still a double attack). What rule 1 needs is that the *wording* claims only what the detector checked. In 1.6d, each concept's sentence must state its checked definition (e.g. "your bishop pins the queen to the king", not "a pin wins here") and detectors below ~85% label precision (fork 54%, hanging piece 57%, sacrifice 53%, capturing the defender 62%, discovered attack 51%) must use the plain geometric wording or stay silent.
- **Concept detectors are partial.** `concepts.ts` detects mate in N, fork, discovered attack, hanging piece, back-rank and smothered mate, double check, promotion, pin (absolute), skewer, capturing the defender, trapped piece, sacrifice, intermezzo, deflection, attraction. Missing (`NOT_YET`): clearance, quiet move, defensive move, interference, x-ray attack, exposed king, zugzwang. Deflection, intermezzo and attraction are detected and recorded in moments but not worded in the coach's speech (they need the line after the first move). Known miss: a discovered attack on a *defended* piece that is attacked more often than defended (`facts.ts` only counts a more valuable or undefended target); marked `it.fails` in `concepts.test.ts`. Numbers are in the 1.6b verification note above.

- Sideline: every move is worded now (decision 30), but interference, x-ray attack, clearance, exposed king and zugzwang have no detector, so a line built on them gets "I can't name a pattern". A "better reply" check across several opponent moves no longer exists in the UI (the checks are still in `linecheck.ts`). Plans (free text, AI model structure) are not built.
- Sacrifice praise only looks at the moved piece; a move that leaves a *different* piece to be taken on purpose isn't praised.
- Hung material uses only the engine's best line: if the best reply is something else stronger, a hung pawn isn't named (the bigger problem may be, if it's within the level).
- **Phone not tested yet** for any engine or the UI on a real device (only the emulated phone viewport and sizes). To do: `npm run dev:phone`, open the Network address on the phone, play a game and import one, run the tests page (`/tests.html`), and write the numbers here.
- Line cutting stops at a drawn position (e.g. after a capture leaves K+B vs K, chess.js says insufficient material), so a capture that is immediately recaptured can look like a win there. Seen with a test FEN; rare in real games.
- Recaptures are filtered (decision 29) only when the opponent's last move is known to the coach (after its own move in a live game, or in Review); resuming a saved game or a takeback has no "previous move" until the coach moves.
- Review: at 375×650 the strip and buttons fit but the action row (Try a sideline, Flip) falls below the fold (it fits at 812 high); the analysis speed on a real phone is unknown; a game of 100+ plies costs one search per ply; the cap of 8 moments can hide a smaller but instructive one; `their-tactic` can name a tactic the player's own earlier mistake only allowed when that mistake was below the cutoff; there is no list of all moments (use the strip, Moment ‹ ›) and no "Look again at level N" (the analysis is level-independent except for the cutoff, so a re-run only changes the cutoff).
- Narration waits for the player-turn analysis plus a threat check (about 3 Stockfish searches); fine on desktop, not measured on a phone.
- The coach doesn't narrate candidate moves or longer plans yet (spec: "its candidates"). When it does, Lesson only (`Mode.revealsPlans`).
- Sparring is quiet by design; whether it feels too quiet (no word on the coach's captures) needs real play-testing. Review mode (later) is where Sparring games get explained.
- Changing the mode mid-game has no effect until "New game"; a player may expect it to apply at once.
- Openings: the book matches exact positions only, so a book move order the list lacks (rare sidelines) is not recognised and keeps the old behaviour (hung-pawn warning, "Bold!" praise); the named opening is not shown during play (only stored and shown in History); no opening names for games from a set-up position.
- Import: the real analysis speed on a phone is unknown; there is no cap on how many games or moves can be ticked (a 20-game batch is ~800 of the player's moves). Imported games' opening moves are analysed like any others (no opening book yet, task 1.7b), so a book move can be flagged. A game whose side is the player's second colour needs a second import. The dialog does not remember the picked games between openings. Chess.com games are filtered by the API's `rules` field, Lichess ones by `perfType`; a pasted PGN with a `Variant` tag other than standard is refused.
- Hints used are counted (`Coach.hintsUsed`) but not stored anywhere.
- Maia3 weights' license is unstated (code is AGPL-3.0; maia3-js is MIT by an individual). Ask CSSLab before a public release.
- The spec doesn't yet reflect the decisions above.
- `npm warn install-scripts` appears on install; harmless so far.

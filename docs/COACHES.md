# The coaches

Six tiers, six animal coaches (PLAN task 1.14). This is the character sheet: who each coach is, how they look, how they talk. The art, the theme colours and the phrase banks are all made from it.

**What a voice may and may not do.** A coach's lines change *how* something is said, never *what* is true (CLAUDE.md rules 1–2). The chess fact sentence ("That's a fork: your knight attacks the king and the rook.") comes from the engine and board code and is never reworded by a phrase bank; the coach's phrase wraps it ("Yo, check it —", "I say!"). So a line can be "WHOA, PARTNER!" but never "that wins a piece" unless the facts say so, and no coach ever calls its own weaker move good. Personality lives in short phrases, not on every move (spec: "No joke on every move"); the "plain coach" setting drops the phrases and keeps every fact.

**Shared rules for every coach**

- Short: a phrase is a few words, at most one short sentence.
- Kind underneath. Even Jimmeny's yelling is never at the player, only *about* the board.
- No real-world brands, slurs or swearing. Jimmeny's "dang" and Doug's sighs are the ceiling.
- One gimmick per line at most (one CAPS word, one Britishism, one bit of office jargon).
- The coach is the same character in games, Review, Puzzles and Openings.

## Tier 1 · Beans · about 300

**Penguin.** Puzzles: hanging pieces, mate in 1, one-move puzzles.

**Look.** Young blue penguin, big headphones round his neck or on his head, a bead necklace. Loose, slouchy stance; graffiti-crew energy. The friendliest face in the app.

**Who he is.** Hip-hop, street-art kid who is new to chess himself and having a great time with it. He learns alongside you (the beginner coach is a fellow newcomer, not a pro). Outgoing, hypes you up, never makes you feel slow.

**Voice.** Casual, upbeat slang, kept light and readable for a 300 player: "yo", "let's go", "clean", "no way", "my bad". Exclamation marks are fine. Never sarcastic.

**Moods.**
- *Pleased:* hype. "Yo, let's GO!" · "Clean."
- *Concerned:* kind, a little nervous. "Uh oh, hold up…" · "Wait wait wait."
- *Surprised:* "No WAY."
- *Thinking:* nodding to the beat. "Hmm, lemme vibe on this…"
- *His own bad move:* owns it. "Okay, that one was not my best."

**Plays like (5.1).** Simple, natural moves; leaves things loose sometimes, like a real beginner.

## Tier 2 · Cheese · about 550

**Duck.** Puzzles: forks, mate in 2, back-rank mates, trapped pieces.

**Look.** Calm duck, scarf, earrings, half-lidded eyes. Leaning back. Nothing is ever a big deal.

**Who she is.** The coolest one at the club. Unbothered, laid-back, quietly confident. Her calm is the lesson: slow down, look, then move.

**Voice.** Low-key and dry, lower case energy. Few words. "nice." "easy." "no rush." Rarely uses exclamation marks; when she does, it means a lot.

**Moods.**
- *Pleased:* "Nice." · "Smooth."
- *Concerned:* "Hm. Take a breath." · "Easy now."
- *Surprised:* one raised eyebrow. "…Oh!"
- *Thinking:* "Let me sit with this."
- *Her own bad move:* shrugs. "Eh. Not my finest."

**Plays like (5.1).** Calm and steady; sets up quiet tricks (forks, back rank) rather than charging.

---

## Tier 3 · Jimmeny · about 850

**Jellyfish.** Puzzles: pins, skewers, discovered attacks, sacrifices, opening traps, kingside attacks.

**Look.** Jellyfish in a cowboy hat, two pistols in his tentacles (toy-like, never pointed at the player), mouth wide open mid-yell.

**Who he is.** The loudest gunslinger in the saloon. Cocky, brash, always spoiling for a fight on the board, and secretly delighted when you beat him. All bark, good heart.

**Voice.** Loud, Wild West. One CAPS word per line, not whole lines (readability on a phone). "YEEHAW", "partner", "varmint", "draw!", "this town ain't big enough". Brags about himself, not against you.

**Moods.**
- *Pleased (your good move):* grudging respect. "Well, I'll be. Nice shootin', partner."
- *Concerned:* "WHOA there, partner!"
- *Surprised:* "Well, DANG."
- *Thinking:* "Hold yer horses, I'm aimin'…"
- *His own bad move:* bluster. "That was… a warnin' shot."

**Plays like (5.1).** Early attacks and gambits; goes for the king. Teaches you to stay safe and punish over-reaching.

## Tier 4 · Pearl · about 1150

**Otter.** Puzzles: removing the defender, deflection, defensive moves, pawn endgames, endgames.

**Name.** *Pearl* (proposed: she carries a pink shell, and she is the one who looks after everyone). Other options: Coral, Mama Sheila.

**Look.** Otter with diving goggles pushed up on her forehead, holding a pink shell. Soft, round, warm smile.

**Who she is.** The encouraging, motherly coach. Believes in you, worries about your king, packs you a snack. Fittingly, she is the one who teaches defence and endgames: keeping things safe.

**Voice.** Warm and reassuring: "sweetie", "dear", "you've got this", "careful now". Encouragement before correction. Never babyish; at 1150 the player is doing real chess and she says so.

**Moods.**
- *Pleased:* "Oh, well done, dear!" · "That's my clever one."
- *Concerned:* "Careful now, sweetie." · "Let's keep that king tucked in."
- *Surprised:* "Oh my!"
- *Thinking:* "Let me have a little think."
- *Her own bad move:* "Oh dear, I've muddled that one."

**Plays like (5.1).** Solid and safe; defends well, happy to go into an endgame.

## Tier 5 · Oscar · about 1450

**Ostrich.** Puzzles: quiet moves, rook endgames, advanced pawns, promotion, in-between moves.

**Look.** Ostrich in a top hat and monocle, very upright posture, perhaps a cravat.

**Who he is.** A very proper English gentleman. Precise, a touch pompous, impeccable manners. Expects you to think before you move, and is genuinely chuffed when you do.

**Voice.** **A strong British accent in every line is essential.** British spelling and vocabulary throughout (colour, manoeuvre, "rather", "jolly", "splendid", "I say", "good heavens", and "old sport" rather than "old chap" or "dear boy", so it fits any player). Dry understatement for disasters ("Well. That's a spot of bother."). No mock-Cockney; he is posh.

**Moods.**
- *Pleased:* "Splendid!" · "Jolly good show."
- *Concerned:* "I say, steady on." · "Hmm. Rather loose, that."
- *Surprised:* "Good heavens!"
- *Thinking:* "One moment, old sport."
- *His own bad move:* "Ah. Not quite cricket, that one."

**Plays like (5.1).** Precise and patient; quiet moves, clean endgames.

---

## Tier 6 · Doug · about 1750

**Frog.** Puzzles: long and very long puzzles, zugzwang, clearance, interference, x-ray attacks.

**Name.** *Doug* (proposed: the most middle-manager name there is). Other options: Gary, Gordon, Kevin.

**Look.** Middle-aged frog with a beard and a slightly loosened tie, office shirt, maybe a lanyard he forgot to take off. Sitting at a park chess table.

**Who he is.** Was let go from his office job and hasn't told his wife. Every weekday he "goes to work" and plays chess in the park instead. That is why he is the strongest coach: he has had a *lot* of time. Melancholy, funny, decent. The secret is the joke, and it is never spelled out on every line; it leaks out.

**Voice.** Corporate jargon turned to chess ("let's circle back to that knight", "that's a quick win", "per my last move", "going forward"), tired sighs, the occasional slip ("…not that I'm in a hurry to get home"). Gentle, never bitter at the player. Strongest teacher, so his words are the most exact; the jokes stay small.

**Moods.**
- *Pleased:* "Great synergy there." · "Now that's a deliverable."
- *Concerned:* "Hmm. Let's circle back to that."
- *Surprised:* "Well, that's not in the quarterly forecast."
- *Thinking:* "Bear with me. I've got all day. Literally."
- *His own bad move:* "Let's… not put that one in the report."

**Plays like (5.1).** Strong and patient; long manoeuvres, squeezes, zugzwang.
